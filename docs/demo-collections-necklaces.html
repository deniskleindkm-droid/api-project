Note for Dennis: Same three-filter pattern as Rings, but a completely different Style taxonomy -- this collection is ~90% single-pendant-on-adjustable-chain (almost no real chokers, layered sets, or tennis necklaces in our actual inventory), so a shape-based taxonomy like Rings used would've left most categories empty. Instead these 7 are motif/scale-based, built from what the real descriptions and specs actually show: Geometric, Symbolic, Nature-Inspired, Statement, Solitaire, Tassel & Drop, Layered & Beaded. Cross-checked against Silverbene's own published necklace taxonomy (Chain, Pendant, Tennis, Locket, and Engrave are their real categories) and 2025-26 market data. Occasion reuses the same 5-tag vocabulary as Rings and Bracelets -- one consistent mood language across the site. All 39 necklaces got a real Style and Occasion tag; none were left untagged this time.

Audit pass (done proactively this time, not after the fact): learned from the Rings session that marketing copy lies about visual boldness, so before finalizing I checked the two largest/riskiest buckets against real photos, not just text. Geometric (8 of 13 checked): found a "Six Pointed Star" pendant that's actually a Star of David -- a real religious symbol, moved to Symbolic, not an abstract shape -- and a "Geometric Beaded Pendant" that's actually an organic freeform nugget shape, moved to Nature. Statement (5 of 5 checked): a novelty shovel-shaped charm and a delicate diamond-by-the-yard station necklace were both demoted out of Statement -- same "copy says bold, photo says delicate" trap as Rings' Statement bucket. Final Statement count dropped from 5 to 3 after these corrections. The other 5 categories were tagged from real specs/descriptions but not given the same full photo audit.

Unlike Rings, the stone-filter gap doesn't apply here -- Necklaces has real inventory in all four stone types (Moissanite, Pearl, Cubic Zirconia, Gemstones), so all four Discover by Stone tiles are reachable.
