Multilingual infrastructure

Language is part
of the architecture.

Keep the source, accepted translation and spoken form distinct, so each can be reviewed, reused and improved independently.

Source language
The language of the original material, preserved as the reference.
Translation language
The language an accepted translation is rendered into.
Interface language
The language used by the application and its controls.
Spoken language
The language the listener hears in generated audio.
Pronunciation language
How names, branded terms and specialist vocabulary are spoken.
Locale
Regional conventions that shape content and delivery.
Original source→Translation candidate→Translation QA→Accepted translation→Spoken form→TTS→Verification

Preserve original material and translation provenance. Reuse accepted translations, enforce organizational terminology, and maintain pronunciation dictionaries for names and branded terms. Regenerate localized audio or replace a TTS provider without retranslating the source.

03 / Control by design

Autonomy has a boundary.
Reliability has a process.

We design what AI may do, how its actions are checked and when a person takes over. Those decisions are part of the architecture.

Define authority→Verify state→Act within limits→Record the result→Escalate exceptions

Explicit authority

Each agent gets a defined role, permitted actions and approval requirements.

Verified actions

Critical paths check authoritative data and current workflow state.

Human judgment

Uncertain or high-impact decisions have a clear escalation path.

Traceable results

Logs, bounded retries and monitoring help explain outcomes and recover failures.

A closer look at the architecture

Like a body, the system has specialists, a coordinating layer and signals between them. Each component has a clear responsibility; the owner stays connected through decisions and exceptions.

  1. Human interfaceReview, decisions and oversight
  2. ARIAMemory, coordination and escalation
  3. Signal layerEvents and state changes
  4. Specialist agentsWork inside explicit authority
  5. Business systemsAPIs, databases and applications
